Every food site makes promises. Ours are deliberately narrow.

BiblicalMeal publishes around three kinds of knowledge, and we keep them
visibly separate wherever they appear. First, **scripture**: what ancient texts
name, bless or forbid. This is textual information, quoted carefully and cited
by book, chapter and verse. Second, **history**: what scholars believe people
of the ancient Mediterranean world grew, traded and ate, based on evidence
such as archaeobotany, trade records and classical writing. Third, **modern
cooking**: what works in a contemporary kitchen today.

When an ingredient page shows scripture references, that is the first kind of
knowledge. When it shows historical context, that claim will always rest on
sources we have reviewed — never on atmosphere. And when a recipe carries our
name, it is exactly what the recipe card says it is: a modern dish inspired by
biblical-world ingredients, unless the page explicitly states otherwise.

This discipline slows us down. An ingredient profile stays marked as _in
research_ — sometimes for months — while we chase down primary material and
decide how confident we can honestly be. You will see those badges across this
site today. They are not decoration; they are our quality control running in
public.

The reward is trust. When BiblicalMeal tells you something about figs, olives
or a bowl of lentil stew, you should be able to see where the statement comes
from — and everything else stays clearly labelled as inspiration, not fact.
That is the standard this whole project is built on, from the first seed entry
to the last.
