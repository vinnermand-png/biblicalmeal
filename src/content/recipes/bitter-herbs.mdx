## About This Recipe

Bitter herbs (Hebrew: _marorim_) are commanded as part of the Passover
meal in Exodus 12:8: "And they shall eat the flesh in that night, roast
with fire, and unleavened bread; and with bitter herbs they shall eat it."

> "And they shall eat the flesh in that night, roast with fire, and
> unleavened bread; and with bitter herbs they shall eat it." — Exodus
> 12:8 (KJV)

The Hebrew word _marorim_ is a category term for bitter plants — not a
specific botanical identification. No single plant is definitively
established by the biblical text as "the" bitter herb. This recipe
presents a selection of available bitter greens as a modern interpretation
of the biblical command.

## Historical Context

Bitter herbs are commanded as part of the Passover meal in Exodus 12:8 and
the command is reinforced in Numbers 9:11 for the second Passover. The
Hebrew word _marorim_ is a plural form of _maror_ (bitter), functioning as
a category term for bitter plants rather than a specific botanical
identification.

The Mishnah (Pesachim 2:6) later lists specific plants — including
chicory, endive, and others — but these are post-biblical rabbinical
traditions, not proof of the original identification. Archaeological and
botanical evidence confirms that several bitter plants grew wild in the
region and would have been available during the spring Passover season.

## Classification

This recipe is classified as **scripture-inspired** — a modern preparation
honouring the Passover command for bitter herbs (Exodus 12:8).

**Important:** This recipe does NOT assert any single plant as "the
biblical bitter herb." The Hebrew word _marorim_ is a category term for
bitter plants. The specific plant identification remains genuinely
uncertain. The Mishnaic list represents post-biblical rabbinical
tradition, not direct biblical evidence.

Any selection of bitter greens is a modern interpretation of the biblical
command — not a historical reconstruction of the original Passover bitter
herbs.

## Evidence Notes

- **Bitter herbs commanded:** Directly attested (Exodus 12:8, Numbers 9:11)
- **_marorim_ as category:** Inferred (plural form suggests selection)
- **Specific plant identification:** Unresolved (not specified in biblical text)
- **Mishnaic plant list:** Post-biblical tradition (not direct biblical evidence)
- **Modern serving preparation:** Practical adaptation

## Serving Notes

Serve as part of a Passover meal alongside unleavened bread, roasted meat,
and other traditional foods. The bitterness of the greens is a reminder of
the Passover story — the bitterness of slavery in Egypt.
