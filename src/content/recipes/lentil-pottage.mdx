## About This Recipe

Lentil pottage (Hebrew: _nazid_) is one of the earliest recorded cooked
dishes in the Hebrew Bible. Genesis 25:29-34 describes Jacob cooking "a
pottage of lentiles" which Esau exchanged for his birthright:

> "And Jacob sod pottage: and Esau came from the field, and he was faint.
> And Esau said to Jacob, Feed me, I pray thee, with that same red
> pottage; for I am faint... Thus Esau despised his birthright." — Genesis
> 25:29-34 (KJV)

This recipe is a historically informed reconstruction. It preserves the
biblical concept of a thick lentil stew while acknowledging that only
lentils are explicitly named in the text. All additional ingredients are
modern practical choices.

## Historical Context

Lentil pottage was a recognised, everyday domestic preparation in the
patriarchal period. The Hebrew word _nazid_ (pottage) suggests a thick,
stewed preparation rather than a thin soup. The adjective _adom_ (red)
applied to Jacob's pottage likely refers to the reddish-brown colour of
cooked lentils.

Lentils are among the oldest cultivated legumes. Archaeological evidence
from Neolithic Galilee sites (pre-Pottery Neolithic B, c. 8000-6000 BCE)
confirms their cultivation in the region. 2 Samuel 17:28-29 mentions
lentils among provisions brought to David's people, and Ezekiel 4:9
includes lentils in a siege bread mixture.

The Genesis text mentions only lentils and the colour red. No other
ingredients — onions, garlic, oil, salt, herbs — are specified in the
biblical account. Any additional ingredients in this recipe are modern
additions chosen for flavour.

## Serving Notes

Serve warm with bread for dipping — a combination that echoes the ancient
practice of eating pottage with bread. The thick, hearty texture is
consistent with the biblical description of _nazid_. This pottage pairs
naturally with the Unleavened Bread recipe, reflecting the simple,
sustaining meals of the biblical world.
