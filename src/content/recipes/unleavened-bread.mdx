## About This Recipe

Unleavened bread (Hebrew: _matzah_) is one of the most explicitly commanded
foods in the Hebrew Bible. Exodus 12:8 requires it alongside the Passover
lamb:

> "And they shall eat the flesh in that night, roast with fire, and
> unleavened bread; and with bitter herbs they shall eat it." — Exodus 12:8
> (KJV)

Deuteronomy 16:3 reinforces the command during the Feast of Unleavened
Bread:

> "Seven days shalt thou eat unleavened bread therewith... that thou mayest
> remember the day when thou camest forth out of the land of Egypt." —
> Deuteronomy 16:3 (KJV)

This recipe is a historically informed reconstruction. It preserves the
biblical concept of bread made without leavening while acknowledging that
the exact flour, quantities, and cooking method are not specified in the
text.

## Historical Context

The biblical text specifies the absence of leavening but does not provide
exact flour quantities, water ratios, or cooking instructions for preparing
the bread itself. Archaeological evidence from ancient Near Eastern sites
confirms that flat, unleavened bread was commonly prepared on heated stones
or in simple clay ovens across the region.

The absence of leavening is the defining characteristic. In the Passover
context, this represents urgency and dependence on God — the Israelites
departed Egypt so quickly that their bread did not have time to rise.

The exact type of flour intended by the Passover command is not specified
in the Exodus text. Some traditions associate matzah with wheat flour, but
barley unleavened bread is also historically attested.

## Classification

This recipe is classified as **historically informed reconstruction** — it
preserves the biblical concept while acknowledging that specific details
remain unresolved.

- **Unleavened bread concept:** Directly attested in Exodus 12:8 and

- **Flour type:** Unresolved — wheat, barley, or mixed grain are all

- **Cooking method:** Inferred from archaeological evidence (heated stones,
  clay ovens)
- **Quantities and proportions:** Modern adaptation — not specified in the
  biblical text

4. How does the Passover matzah tradition relate to everyday unleavened

These questions are about modern adaptation choices, not missing historical
evidence. The biblical command is clear; the practical details of
implementation are where uncertainty remains.

## Serving Notes

Serve warm alongside stews, soups, or dips. The absence of leavening gives
these flatbreads a dense, chewy texture — this is historically consistent
with the biblical description of unleavened bread. The bread pairs
naturally with the Lentil Pottage recipe, echoing the ancient practice of
eating stewed lentils with bread.
