## About This Recipe

Fish is one of the most explicitly attested foods in the Gospel narratives.
Luke 24:42-43 records the risen Jesus eating broiled fish with his
disciples — the most direct explicit eating reference for Jesus in the
Gospels.

> "And they gave him a piece of a broiled fish, and of an honeycomb. And he
> took it, and did eat before them." — Luke 24:42-43 (KJV)

This recipe honors that ancient tradition as a modern kitchen creation. It
is not a historical reconstruction of a specific ancient preparation.

## Historical Context

Fish was a staple of the Galilean economy. The Sea of Galilee was a major
fishing center, and archaeological evidence confirms extensive fishing
operations in the first century. Fish bones, weights, hooks, and net
fragments have been found at multiple sites around the Sea of Galilee,
including at Magdala, Capernaum, and Ginosar.

Multiple Gospel passages reference fish in the context of meals: the
calling of the first disciples (who were fishermen), the feeding of the
five thousand (which involved loaves and fishes), and post-resurrection
fishing encounters.

The specific preparation in Luke 24 is "broiled" (Greek: _optos_ —
roasted, baked, or cooked over fire), suggesting a simple open-fire or
stone preparation common in the region.

## Classification

This recipe is classified as **scripture-inspired** — a modern kitchen
creation that draws on the explicitly attested fish consumption in the
Gospels without claiming to replicate a specific historical preparation.

The Greek word _optos_ (Luke 24:42) suggests roasted or cooked over fire,
which is consistent with the grilling approach used here. However, the
specific fish species, seasonings, and modern cooking equipment are all
contemporary choices.

## Evidence Notes

- **Fish consumption:** Directly attested in Luke 24:42-43
- **Grilling/broiling method:** Inferred from the Greek word _optos_
- **Fish species:** Not specified in the text; modern choice
- **Seasonings:** Modern adaptations (olive oil, cumin, lemon, parsley)

## Serving Notes

Serve immediately while warm. This recipe pairs well with simple bread and
fresh vegetables — a combination that echoes the everyday meals of the
biblical world.
