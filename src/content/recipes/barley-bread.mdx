instructions:
  - Combine the warm water, honey and yeast in a bowl. Let stand for 5
    minutes until slightly foamy.
  - In a large bowl, mix the barley flour, all-purpose flour and salt. Add
    the yeast mixture and olive oil. Stir until a shaggy dough forms.
  - Turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface and knead for 8-10
    minutes, until smooth and elastic.
  - Place the dough in a lightly oiled bowl, cover with a damp cloth, and
    let rise in a warm spot for about 1 hour, until doubled in size.
  - Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F). Shape the dough into a round loaf
    or divide into 6-8 smaller rolls.
  - Bake for 25-30 minutes, until golden brown and hollow-sounding when
    tapped. Let cool slightly before serving.

## About This Recipe

Barley is one of the seven species of the Land of Israel, named alongside
wheat, vines, figs, pomegranates, olive oil, and honey in Deuteronomy 8:8.

> "A land of wheat, and barley, and vines, and fig trees, and pomegranates;
> a land of oil olive, and honey." — Deuteronomy 8:8 (KJV)

Barley loaves appear in the feeding of the five thousand (John 6:9), where
a boy offers five barley loaves and two small fishes to Jesus.

> "There is a lad here, which hath five barley loaves, and two small
> fishes: but what are they among so many?" — John 6:9 (KJV)

This recipe honors the ancient tradition of barley bread as a modern
kitchen creation.

## Historical Context

Barley (Hebrew: _se'orah_) was the staple grain of common people in the
ancient Near East — cheaper and coarser than wheat bread. Archaeological
evidence confirms barley cultivation in the region from the Neolithic
period onward.

Barley bread was likely prepared as simple flatbreads or thick cakes,
cooked on heated stones or in clay ovens. The texture would have been
dense and coarse compared to wheat bread.

The biblical text references barley in multiple contexts: Ruth gleaning
barley (Ruth 2), the miracle of the barley loaves (John 6:9, 13), and
barley as animal feed (1 Kings 4:28).

## Classification

This recipe is classified as **scripture-inspired** — a modern kitchen
creation that draws on the attested use of barley for bread without
claiming to replicate a specific historical preparation.

The biblical text confirms barley bread existed (John 6:9) and that barley
was a primary grain (Deuteronomy 8:8), but it does not provide a specific
bread-making recipe. The flour type, proportions, leavening, and cooking
method used here are modern choices.

## Evidence Notes

- **Barley as bread grain:** Directly attested in Deuteronomy 8:8 and
  John 6:9
- **Bread-making method:** Inferred from ancient Near Eastern practices
- **Flour type and proportions:** Modern adaptation
- **Leavening (yeast):** Modern convenience; whether ancient barley breads
  were leavened is not specified

## Serving Notes

Serve warm with olive oil for dipping, or alongside stews and soups. The
dense, nutty flavor of barley bread pairs well with the hearty
preparations of the ancient Near East. Barley bread is coarser than wheat
bread — this is historically consistent, as barley was the bread of common
people.
