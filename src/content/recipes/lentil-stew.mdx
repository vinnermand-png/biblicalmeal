## About This Recipe

This lentil stew is a BiblicalMeal kitchen creation — a modern tribute to the
ancient pantry, not a reconstruction of any single historical dish. It draws
inspiration from the long tradition of lentil cooking that stretches back to
the earliest agricultural communities of the ancient Near East.

Lentils appear in the Bible in several contexts: Jacob cooking "pottage of
lentiles" for which Esau traded his birthright (Genesis 25:29-34), lentiles
among provisions brought to David's people (2 Samuel 17:28-29), and lentils
included in Ezekiel's siege bread (Ezekiel 4:9). Each of these passages
presents lentils in its own narrative setting — this recipe is not a
reconstruction of any one of them.

## Historical Connection

The Genesis 25 passage places lentil cooking in a domestic context, suggesting
that stewed lentils were a recognized food in the ancient world. Archaeological
evidence from Neolithic Galilee sites supports the antiquity of lentil
cultivation in the region.

This stew draws on that long tradition while using ingredients and methods
available in a modern kitchen. The result is a dish that honors the ancient
connection between lentils and sustenance without claiming to replicate any
specific historical preparation.

## Method

1. **Warm the olive oil** in a heavy pot over medium heat. Add the onion and
   carrots and cook, stirring occasionally, until softened — about 6–8
   minutes.

2. **Stir in the garlic and cumin** and cook for one minute, until fragrant.

3. **Add the lentils, crushed tomatoes, stock and bay leaf.** Bring to a
   boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer.

4. **Simmer uncovered** for 35–45 minutes, stirring now and then, until the
   lentils are tender and the stew has thickened. Add a splash of water if
   it becomes too dense.

5. **Season with salt and pepper.** Remove the bay leaf.

6. **Finish with lemon juice and parsley.** Rest five minutes off the heat
   and serve warm, ideally with bread for dipping.

## Serving Notes

This stew pairs well with crusty bread for dipping — a combination that echoes
the ancient practice of serving stews with bread. The lemon juice at the end
adds brightness that balances the earthy lentils and warm spices.
