    quantity: 2 cups (250 g)
  - item: Salt
    quantity: 1/2 tsp
  - item: Warm water
    quantity: 3/4 cup (180 ml)
  - item: Olive oil
    quantity: 2 tbsp, plus extra for brushing
instructions:
  - Combine the flour and salt in a bowl. Make a well in the centre and pour
    in the warm water and olive oil. Stir until a soft dough forms.
  - Turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface and knead for 5-6 minutes,
    until smooth.
  - Divide the dough into 8 equal pieces. Roll each into a ball, then flatten
    into thin rounds, about 15 cm (6 inches) across.
  - Heat a dry frying pan or skillet over medium-high heat. Cook each
    flatbread for 1-2 minutes per side, until puffed and lightly charred.
  - Stack the cooked flatbreads and cover with a clean cloth to keep soft.
    Serve warm.

## About This Recipe

Wheat is the first species named in Deuteronomy 8:8:

> "A land of wheat, and barley, and vines, and fig trees, and pomegranates;
> a land of oil olive, and honey." — Deuteronomy 8:8 (KJV)

Wheat was the premium grain of the ancient Near East — finer and more
valued than barley bread. Flatbread (Hebrew: _uggah_) was the most common
bread form, simple rounds of dough cooked on heated stones or in clay ovens.

This recipe honors that ancient tradition as a modern kitchen creation.

## Historical Context

Wheat (Hebrew: _chittah_) is one of the seven species of the Land of
Israel and the premium grain of the ancient Near East. Archaeological
evidence confirms wheat cultivation in the Levant from the Pre-Pottery
Neolithic period (c. 9000 BCE).

The biblical text references wheat frequently: the manna was compared to
coriander seed (Exodus 16:31), wheat appears in economic and agricultural
contexts throughout the Old Testament, and Jesus uses wheat in parables
(Matthew 13:24-30, the parable of the wheat and tares).

Flatbread was the most common bread form — simple rounds of dough cooked
on heated stones, in clay ovens, or over open fire.

## Classification

This recipe is classified as **scripture-inspired** — a modern kitchen
creation that draws on the attested use of wheat for bread without claiming
to replicate a specific historical preparation.

The biblical text confirms wheat was used for bread (Deuteronomy 8:8) and
that flatbread was common in the ancient Near East, but it does not provide
a specific flatbread recipe. The flour type, proportions, and cooking
method used here are modern choices.

## Evidence Notes

- **Wheat as bread grain:** Directly attested in Deuteronomy 8:8
- **Flatbread tradition:** Inferred from ancient Near Eastern practices
- **Flour type and proportions:** Modern adaptation
- **Cooking method (skillet):** Modern convenience; ancient flatbreads were
  likely cooked on heated stones or in clay ovens

## Serving Notes

Serve warm with olive oil, alongside stews, or with dips. The soft, pliable
texture makes these flatbreads versatile — use them to scoop up food, wrap
fillings, or tear and share at the table. This recipe does not use
leavening, making it similar to the unleavened bread tradition (Exodus 12),
though it is not presented as Passover matzah.
