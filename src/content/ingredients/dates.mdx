## Biblical References

The verified KJV evidence currently concerns palm trees and palm branches.
These references establish the recorded biblical palm context, but this
content does not treat every palm reference as automatic proof of edible-date
consumption.

**Exodus 15:27** reports that Elim had seventy palm trees. This passage
establishes the presence of palm trees in the wilderness itinerary, though
edible dates are not named.

**Leviticus 23:40** commands branches of palm trees for the feast. This
ritual context concerns branches and rejoicing, not edible fruit.

**Judges 4:5** places Deborah under a palm tree where Israelites came to her
for judgment. This narrative location identifies a palm tree as a landmark,
not dates as food.

**Joel 1:12** includes the palm tree among trees described as withered in a
crop-failure image. This agricultural imagery does not identify edible dates.

**John 12:13** reports people taking branches of palm trees when they went to
meet Jesus. This narrative procession concerns branches, not fruit
consumption.

**Psalm 92:12** compares the flourishing of the righteous to a palm tree.
This poetic comparison does not establish dates as food.

## Historical Context

Historical discussion remains limited to what the reviewed evidence supports.
A biblical reference to a palm can be significant in its own context without
settling the separate question of when a specific reference should be read as
a food reference.

The palm tree appears across wilderness, ritual, narrative, and poetic
contexts in Scripture, each with its own purpose that must be respected
rather than collapsed into a single claim about date consumption.

## Archaeological Evidence

A reviewed Science Advances study analyzes ancient Phoenix dactylifera seeds
recovered from Judean Desert archaeological sites and supports the existence
of a historic Judean date-palm culture. This archaeological evidence does
not, by itself, identify every KJV palm reference as an edible-date
reference.

The archaeological evidence supports ancient date palms and a historic date
culture, while the relationship between that evidence and each biblical palm
reference remains a separate interpretive question.

## Food Use

The present content keeps food use narrower than botanical identity. Palm
reference does not equal edible-date reference, so the edible-date question
remains explicitly qualified wherever the evidence does not establish it
directly.

The date palm's fruit — the date itself — is a concentrated, sweet food that
has been cultivated across the Middle East and North Africa for millennia.
Modern date cultivation connects to this ancient tradition while using
contemporary agricultural methods.

## What Remains Uncertain

The palm-versus-edible-date distinction remains unresolved for the individual
KJV palm references in the current evidence set. The archaeological evidence
supports ancient date palms and a historic date culture, while the
relationship between that evidence and each biblical palm reference remains a
separate interpretive question.

The precise role of dates in the daily diet of ancient Israelites remains
interpretive rather than directly attested from a single source. Each palm
reference must remain bounded by its recorded setting.
