## Biblical References

Lentils appear in three distinct biblical contexts:

**Genesis 25:29-34** records Jacob cooking lentil pottage ("pottage of
lentiles"), for which Esau traded his birthright. This narrative passage
establishes lentils as a recognized food, but the story is primarily about
the birthright transaction, not a recipe endorsement.

**2 Samuel 17:28-29** lists lentiles among the provisions brought to David's
hungry and weary people at Mahanaim. This passage places lentils in a
provision and sustenance context.

**Ezekiel 4:9** prescribes a mixed-grain bread including lentils, eaten by
weight for 390 days as enacted siege conditions. This passage is explicitly
framed as siege symbolism — any future recipe content derived from this verse
must keep the famine framing visible.

## Historical Context

A Weizmann Institute / Israel Antiquities Authority study of Neolithic Galilee
sites found legumes — including lentils alongside fava beans, peas, and
chickpeas — formed a substantial part of the prehistoric diet. This
archaeological evidence supports the antiquity of lentil cultivation in the
region, though the study remains under full review.

The Genesis 25 narrative places lentils in a domestic cooking context, while
the Ezekiel 4 passage connects them with siege conditions. These different
settings should not be collapsed into a single claim about how all biblical
communities used lentils.

## Archaeological Evidence

The Weizmann/IAA study of Neolithic Galilee sites provides direct evidence
for legume cultivation, including lentils. This evidence is based on
archaeological recovery and is reported through the Biblical Archaeology
Society.

Researchers suggested that legumes were among the first plant species
domesticated west of the Jordan River, though this interpretation comes from
a single study and should be treated as plausible rather than settled science.

## What Remains Uncertain

The full archaeological review of the Weizmann/IAA study is pending. While
the secondary reporting supports the antiquity of lentil cultivation, the
precise scope and interpretation of the findings require additional
verification before any broad historical claims are made.

The relationship between the Genesis 25 narrative and actual historical
cooking practices remains interpretive rather than directly attested.

## Related Foods

The lentil connects naturally to the broader legume and pulse family in the
ancient Mediterranean diet, and bridges to related BiblicalMeal content on
barley, wheat, and other staples of the ancient table.
