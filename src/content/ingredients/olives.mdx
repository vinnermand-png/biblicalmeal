## Biblical References

The olive appears across several distinct contexts in the KJV:

**Deuteronomy 8:8** names the olive among the land's signature products,
rendered "oil olive" in the KJV. This passage establishes the olive as part
of the agricultural identity of the promised land.

**Genesis 8:11** reports the dove returning to Noah with a freshly plucked
olive leaf, marking the end of the flood. This narrative context presents the
olive tree as a sign of renewal and new growth.

**Micah 6:15** depicts olive processing as treading the olives, paired with
losing the anointing oil as covenant-curse imagery. The treading-to-oil
process is presented as expected agricultural practice.

**Deuteronomy 24:20** commands beating the olive harvest once, leaving what
remains for the stranger, fatherless, and widow. This passage connects olive
harvesting with social responsibility.

**Judges 9:8-9** pictures the olive tree declining kingship because its
"fatness" honors God and man. This parable material presents the olive's
value in symbolic and theological terms.

## Historical Context

The KJV "oil olive" rendering of Deuteronomy 8:8 carries an important scope
question: the passage names the tree, but the culinary and daily-life
significance of olive oil is a separate historical question that requires its
own evidence.

The olive's appearance across agricultural, harvest, and covenant contexts
suggests it was deeply integrated into ancient Israelite life, but each
passage must be read in its own narrative setting rather than collapsed into a
single universal claim about olive use.
